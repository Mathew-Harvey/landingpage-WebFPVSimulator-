Betaflight 4.5.1 settings reference

P yaw

CLI setting p_yaw. Works here. Parameter group PID_PROFILE. Firmware type UINT8. Range 0 to 250.

What it does

P yaw is the proportional gain for the yaw axis. When the quad is yawing faster or slower than you asked, P changes the power to the clockwise and anticlockwise pairs of motors in proportion to the difference. It is the setting pilots usually mean when they call a quad snappy or lazy.

How it works

Betaflight's PID code calculates the yaw P term as the P gain multiplied by the error: the target yaw rate minus the filtered gyro rate. The gain is in Betaflight's own units, not SI units. P has no memory of earlier errors, so a disturbance that lasts, such as the small yaw drift that the motor cant causes in a hover, is corrected by the I term.

In this simulator

Works here. It sets the yaw P gain in Betaflight's PID profile. The physics model never reads it: it only receives the motor power levels that come out of the mixer.

If you raise it

Yaw starts sooner, and small movements of the left stick to the left and right feel more directly connected to the quad. Past a certain point the quad buzzes, especially when you add throttle quickly and the gyro vibration is strongest.

A higher P corrects errors faster and shortens the delay between the stick and the quad. It also magnifies any vibration that gets past the gyro filters, and the quad settles less smoothly after each movement, so D has to do more work to stop overshoot. On yaw, raising P is often a better choice than raising D, because the yaw D signal is noisy.

If you lower it

Yaw feels soft and late. You wait for the quad to respond, then move the stick too far.

A lower P takes longer to correct a rate error, and the I term builds up more in the meantime. Feedforward can hide a low P during stick movements, but the quad still recovers more slowly from a disturbance.
