# CLAUDE.md

Project conventions. Read fully before any turn. These are decisions already made, not options.

## What this is

The front door for WebFPVSimulator. One canvas, one scroll, six acts, a reason, and four links out. It owns no data and calls no API: the launch buttons are links, and the origins they point at live in `src/config.js`.

The three repositories are one product. `Mathew-Harvey/WebFPVSimulator` holds the simulator and the track builder and is the copy of record for anything shared; `Mathew-Harvey/WebFPVSimulator-LeaderBoard` is the board. Read the simulator's `CLAUDE.md` before changing anything that has to agree across the three, and `DEPLOY.md` there for how they are wired together.

## Decisions already made

**Licence is GPLv3.** Every file gets a header.

**No dependencies, no build step, no bundler.** Three.js arrives from a CDN import map and nothing else does. Adding anything needs an argument first.

**The scroll is the timeline.** `main.js` maps scroll position to a camera and a set of acts. It must not be hijacked by smooth scrolling: the damping lives in `main.js` where it can be tuned against the camera. Every `[data-act]` element is measured into the timeline, so a new section that is not an act goes outside `<main>`, the way `#why` and `#close` do. Nothing counts the acts by hand: `timeline()` measures them, the ledger is a list, and the eyebrow numbers renumber with the markup. An act inserted in the middle should cost the markup, the ledger row, the copy block and the camera branch, and nothing else.

**The simulator's code is copied, never edited, into `src/sim/`.** `src/sim/` mirrors the simulator's own `src/` layout, so every file arrives byte for byte and no import is rewritten. `node scripts/vendor.js ../WebFPVSimulator` makes it: the import closure of the entries listed in that script, which are the parts of the built map the yard is drawn with, the whoop room's wall art, the lettering the titles are drawn in, and the partners' roster. The pictures those name from outside the simulator's `src/`, the wall art's `assets/wallart/atlas.webp` and the partners' marks under `assets/partners/`, are copied to the same paths from this repository's root, because the modules that name them name them by those paths. `src/sim/MANIFEST.json` records the simulator's commit and a SHA-256 per file, the pictures' included, and `npm run lint:page` fails on any file that does not match. A change the page needs in that code is made in the simulator, which is the copy of record, and recopied.

**The film has no town.** The simulator's town (sakura-crossing) was act four: flown through in the goggles after the lap, then craned over to the yard. The owner took it out on 2026-09-27, because it took ages to load and pulled attention off the freestyle map builder and the custom maps people make with it, which are the point. The lap now dissolves straight onto the map builder's plan. `src/city.js` is gone and nothing imports the town; `scripts/vendor.js` no longer copies it: all that is left under `src/sim/maps/city/vendored/` is the ten files of its shared code the yard imports, with their MIT licence and patch notes, recorded in `NOTICE`. Do not put the town back without the owner asking.

**The yard is the simulator's showpiece, and the freestyle chapter builds it and then flies it.** Hibari Yard Tandem is the simulator's `src/maps/built/showpiece.js`: the starter yard with a drift course and two drift cars in tandem added, published on the board under its fixed id, which `src/config.js` links to. `src/yard-data.js` is GENERATED by `node scripts/bake-yard.js ../WebFPVSimulator > src/yard-data.js`: the document, normalized as the simulator reads it, and every car's lap as the simulator's physics module drives it, one lap each, because a car one lap on is the same car. Regenerate, do not edit. `src/yard.js` is a join: it draws the yard with the simulator's own place, kit, ground, road, car and look modules, leaves out the map's own two kilometre terrain (the page's deck is cut out from under the plot instead), puts each element in a chunk of its own so the builder act can set it down, and owns the line flown: the approach from the pads and the chase, keyed to how far round the chase car is. `window.__wf.yard.clearance()` measures that line against the map's solids and the tandem's bodies; a change to the line is checked with it, not with a screenshot. The tandem is in sync because the simulator makes it so (two cars on one road at one speed share a speed table), and the simulator's `scripts/roads-check.js` holds it; nothing here times them. Where the yard stands is `src/places.js`, turned a quarter so a camera looking down on it has the builder's plan the right way up.

**Nothing heavy is built behind the boot screen.** It lifts on the studio's first frame. The yard and every place's warm pass (shaders compiled with `compileAsync`, buffers uploaded a slice at a time into an eight pixel scissor) are jobs in `src/loader.js`, run a step at a time by the budget in `main.js`: nothing while the visitor scrolls or the opening build plays, a little when the frame is quiet, more when it is still, and everything while a hold is waiting. A place the film reaches before it is built is waited for inside its transition, the dissolve held in haze or the blackout held dark, with a note, never shown half built. The yard is waited for in the lap's dissolve; it is built behind the opening build and the lap and is nearly always long done. A new place is a new job and a new hold, not a longer boot.

**There are exactly two cuts in the film, they go opposite ways, and neither is a hard cut.** Everything else is flown: the studio floor becomes a plan grid, the grid becomes a field, and the quad that leaves the studio arrives at the track. The map builder and the shed are the exceptions, and each one is an exception for its own reason.

The map builder is reached by a **dissolve UP, into warm haze**, out of the lap's goggles and onto the builder's plan, looking straight down on the yard's plot. Nothing flies between a race field and a plot seen from three hundred metres up; the haze carries the lens change and the plan's light.

The shed is reached by a **blackout DOWN, into the dark**, and it is a different device rather than the same one twice. What is on the far side of it is indoors with the lights off, so the frame does not come back by a veil lifting off a lit room: the room's own lights come on, in `setLamps`, and the air comes up with them, and the light arrives in the place rather than on the page. Nothing could be flown between a yard and a shed hundreds of metres away and indoors, and the aircraft changes too, which is a thing a cut can say and a transit cannot.

Both are fades rather than hard cuts because the page is scrubbable: a reader dragging the bar slowly across a hard cut sees it flicker, and a fade has a middle for the camera to move in. Both are symmetric for the same reason. A third transition needs an argument this good, and there is not one: any further place in this film has to be flown to.

**The room is the simulator's room, and the track in it is generated.** `src/room.js` builds the same 10 by 12 by 4 m shed `WebFPVSimulator/src/render/scene.js` builds for a micro track, in the same colours and by the same recipe: uprights whose inner faces are the opening, a cross member above every opening and below only the ones that are off the floor, a moulded corner at each junction, stub feet, and no printing of any kind, because a RaceGOW gate is bare white pipe. A visitor who clicks through from that act lands in that room and the two must not disagree about it.

That room is the simulator's sakura room, recoloured on its owner's ask: a deep green gate band to 1.2 m under a sakura rail, pale plaster above, honey boards round a dark mat, a cream ceiling painted as lit with twelve light panels, four lamps, and the simulator's pale grey pipe, which reads against the band and the plaster alike. The palette and the figures are copied number for number from its `ROOM` block and its lights. The posters and banners on its walls are not drawn here: where each hangs and the quads that hang it are the simulator's `src/art/wallart-hang.js`, copied, and only their lighting is this page's, because the simulator lights them with its `celmat.js` and `src/cel.js` is this page's port of that file, which patches the same shader chunk. Nothing in the room casts a shadow, as nothing in the simulator's does, and the stage parks its shadow camera for the act rather than switching shadows off, which would recompile the page. The room's own furniture is a bench with a pilot's kit and a chair, and nothing that stands against a wall, because the art is there.

`src/room-data.js` is the demo track and is GENERATED, by `node scripts/bake-room.js ../WebFPVSimulator > src/room-data.js`. It reads the simulator's own preset through the simulator's own `courseFromDocument`, path solver and all, so the gates are where the track says and the line is the one the builder derives. A second argument picks a different preset by id, which is how the demo track is changed: never by editing the data. Regenerate, do not edit. What `src/room.js` owns is the shed, the lamps and the furniture; what it must never own is a gate position.

The act's copy now quotes NO measurement, and that is the safest state for it. It used to say three and a half metres by two and fourteen metres of lap; the generator has measured the shipped track at 2.97 by 1.42 with a 13.8 m lap since it was last regenerated, so the front door was advertising a track nobody could build. `LAP_LENGTH` and `SPAN` are still exported and still measured, so a number can come back into the copy whenever it earns its place, and any that does has to be checked against them when the track changes. The generator cannot write the prose, but it can make the prose's numbers findable in one file.

**The room act names RaceGOW, links them, and points at the next season.** Micro UTT is their format, the track in the act is their published Track 1 designed by Skittles, and the simulator ships all eight of Season 5. The act says so in one sentence, links `https://www.racegow.com/` on the name, and carries the sign up for RaceGOW 6 beside the link that flies the track. The series was deliberately unnamed here once, borrowing the board's argument about its whoop plate; that argument is about a heading over everybody's tracks and this is one act flying one of theirs, where leaving the name off is not neutrality. The builder still makes your own, and the copy still says so in the same breath.

**Act one is the partners' strip, the wordmark, the headline, the tagline and the three chapter panels, and nothing else.** The strip over the wordmark is the owner's ask of 2026-09-27 and is described with the partners below. It is laid out as a manga page, below. The chapters (five inch racing, freestyle, whoop) were a section of their own, `#worlds`, headed "Three kinds of track" and set between the build and the track with the film held still behind it; the owner moved them onto the first screen on 2026-09-27, under the headline and over the quad being built, and took the heading, its lede and the "keep scrolling" line away with the section. They are a sibling of the copy in act one's pin, not inside it, because `composeLayout()` aims the studio camera at the space right of the copy's box. They have no ledger row. Act one used to carry two ledes and an admission: a five inch quad built in front of you, Betaflight 4.5.1 linked and thanked, and a dry line saying the page was built with AI. The owner took all three off the first screen on 2026-09-27, so the film opens on the build with nothing to read over it. Betaflight is still named there, in the tagline, named in the FAQ and the metadata, and linked in the footer; the control loop IS Betaflight compiled to WebAssembly and the licence is theirs by descent, so the credit stays wherever the page makes that claim. Do not put the ledes or the admission back without the owner asking.

**The first screen is a manga page, and scrolling opens it into the film.** The owner's brief of 2026-09-27: the game should feel like flying a manga comic, and the first thing a visitor sees has to say so; the page before it was a dark studio with laptop sized type, an island in a void on a 4K monitor. So act one opens as a page in the simulator's own page grammar (its results page, `src/ui/mangapage.js`): paper, ink borders, gutters that lean, the biggest panel first. `src/page.js` is the geometry and nothing else: margin, gutter and border at the simulator's 3.5, 2.2 and 0.65 per cent of the window's short side, one cut across under the big panel and two leaning cuts down through the chapter tier, three shapes by aspect (a spread, a page like the owner's own 1877 by 1938 window, a strip on a phone), one SVG of paper with the panels cut out and one ink line round each, each chapter link clipped to its quad. Everything on the first screen is sized in `--u`, a hundredth of the short side; type keeps a rem term so browser zoom still enlarges it. The big panel is the live studio, and `src/manga.js` draws it as ink on paper for as long as act one lasts: the dome, deck, fog and clear colour to paper, the airframe in inverted hulls (not a depth edge pass: at 4K the hulls gave the same picture for no full screen target), the cast shadow as the simulator's 45 degree dot tone, and focus lines converging on the aircraft. The focus lines are STILL, a seeded hash moved only by the camera, which the scroll moves, and they are held off the aircraft and off every box that has to be read, because a field of stripes that changes on its own is a photosensitivity question; do not give them a clock. While the page is up `main.js` slides the picture with a view offset so the quad sits in what the panel leaves it, which changes no pose, and the slide is gone by the time the page has opened. `--open` (T 0.04 to 0.30) zooms the frame past the glass; the paper goes back to the studio's colours quickly at T 0.90 to 0.965, because paper and maroon have a muddy grey between them. There is no Japanese writing on the page: the owner tried katakana sound effects on the studio and the freestyle panel and took them out. While the studio is paper, `body.on-paper` inks the top bar, the build order and the cue, and the ledger steps aside. Under reduced motion the film is pinned in act three, so the big panel is left as paper, a title page. The chrome (top bar, build order, ledger, cue) is scaled as a whole at 2200 by 1240 and 3000 by 1650 and up, so what was tuned at 1440 stays tuned at 4K.

**No link goes in a beat.** `#beats` is `pointer-events: none`, because it is fixed over the middle of a page whose whole interaction is the scroll, and a beat is on screen for a fifth of an act. A link in one would look clickable and not be. Anything that has to be clicked belongs in an act's copy block, the top bar, or the footer.

**The copy says what a thing is, and nothing else.** A headline, a line under it, and a beat that names what is in frame. No aphorisms, no "every gate is a decision made three gates early", no promise that a keyboard cannot teach you anything. If a sentence would survive being moved to a different product's page, it is not saying anything about this one. The film is the argument; the words are captions for it.

**No brand is named for a controller.** A radio or a game controller, every time, in the invitation, the kit list, the beat and the cards. Naming one manufacturer's pad tells everybody holding a different one that the page was not written for them, and the kit list is the whole pitch.

**The palette is the simulator's.** Light is warm, shadow is cool. Cream for lit type, sakura for chrome, amber for an instrument, mint for something good, slate for type that should recede. The panel fill and the two pixel edge rule are the board's, so a visitor arriving from the board is looking at the same furniture.

**The titles are lettered in the simulator's hand, and the words stay.** The wordmarks (the top bar's, the boot screen's and act one's) and every `h2` are drawn by the simulator's `src/ui/lettering.js`, copied into `src/sim/ui/`: heavy slanted capitals, an ink line, a hard drop and two cel bands, each run in its own computed colour, so an `<em>` is sakura because the stylesheet says so. It is not a font and none ships: the simulator has none, and the lettering is the system's heaviest sans used as a skeleton. `src/titles.js` is the join, as `letterHeading` is in the simulator's menus, with one difference: the simulator's titles fit on a line and this page's wrap, so the browser breaks the lines and each line gets a canvas of its own on its own baseline. It paints when a title's box changes, never per frame, and it is started by a module of its own at the foot of `index.html`, ahead of the film's, because the film's graph begins with three.js from a CDN and the boot screen's wordmark is up for the whole of that fetch; `npm run lint:page` counts it. The text stays in the DOM with its fill made transparent, for a screen reader, find in page and a search engine, and forced colours gets it back. Ledes, eyebrows, cards and beats stay text, because the simulator keeps them as text; the chapter panels' names are lettered, because the simulator's own manga page letters its panels' names. The OSD is inked as the simulator's is, from the `--osd-*` tokens. The wiki letters its wordmark and nothing else: its titles are headings that are read. The slap pack has no module, so its mark is the same hand as vector, drawn into the page with the stickers' wordmarks (see the stickers).

**Reduced motion is honoured in two places that agree.** The CSS block at the foot of the stylesheet and `REDUCED` in `main.js`. Anything that animates on scroll needs an entry in both. Nothing that carries meaning is hidden. The wiki has its own pair of the same contract, the block at the foot of `wiki/index.html` and `REDUCED` in `src/wiki/anim.js`, and `npm run lint:wiki` fails if either half goes missing.

**A wiki figure computes, it does not illustrate.** `src/wiki/model.js` holds the plant's constants, snapshotted from `plant.c` the way `src/fc` snapshots the catalog, and the figures solve with them rather than drawing a shape from memory. If a figure and its article disagree about a number, one of them is a bug. Do not put a number in a caption the figure does not compute, and do not draw a curve the plant does not have: name the absence instead. Every article carries a figure, every figure has a knob with a readout, and the caption says what moving the knob proves.

**The site icon comes from the simulator's `scripts/icons.js`.** `icon.svg`, `favicon.ico` and `apple-touch-icon.png` are generated output, in cream, because the front door owns no product's accent. Regenerate, do not edit: `node scripts/icons.js cream ../landingpage-WebFPVSimulator-` from a checkout of the simulator beside this one.

**The share card is the first screen, drawn by the page.** `og.jpg` is the 1200 by 630 picture every link to this site shows, and it is the manga page at T = 0 once the quad has built itself and the pilot sticker has landed, captured by `node scripts/og.js` in headless Chromium: laid out at 1600 by 840, drawn at twice the card and brought down, a JPEG at 0.92 because WhatsApp is widely reported to drop a preview much past 300 KB. It replaced a composed card, the mark beside a frame of the race field, on the owner's ask of 2026-09-27. Every page in this repository names it, the wiki's articles through `scripts/generate-wiki-pages.js`, at `og.jpg?v=` and the first eight hex digits of its SHA-256, because Facebook and X keep a picture by its address. Regenerate, do not edit: the script writes the card, moves that address on every page that names it and regenerates the wiki's articles, and what is left is to commit and push; `npm run lint:page` fails while any page names another address, size or description, or an address that is not the card's own hash. The script waits on what the page shows, never on a clock, and seeds the invitation as seen and Global Privacy Control on, so a redraw is neither a card of the dialog nor a partner impression. Its type is whatever `system-ui` is on the machine that draws it, Segoe UI on the owner's and DejaVu Sans in a bare container. The simulator's `scripts/og.js` draws the simulator's card and the board's, never this one.

**The stickers are the slap pack's, and the pack is the copy of record.** `stickers/index.html` is one self contained page: twenty two stickers as inline SVG, the faces they are set in as base64 (Zen Kaku Gothic New at two weights and Caveat Brush), and a download for each. Every WEBFPV wordmark a sticker sets in type, and the pack's own mark, is the simulator's lettering drawn as vector: a `<g data-letter>` spot whose attributes are the design and whose contents are GENERATED by `node scripts/letter-stickers.js ../WebFPVSimulator`, which runs the simulator's `drawRuns` against a 2D context that writes SVG. Edit a spot's attributes and regenerate, never the glyphs. Brush handwriting and small print stay drawn by hand. The simulator's room is hung with wall art made from the pack, so a change to a sticker it uses is followed by `node scripts/wallart.js --pack=` this checkout's pack in the simulator, and the atlas recopied here. The film wears eight of them, slapped on the glass rather than laid on the page, and every one is the actual sticker: `src/stickers-data.js` is GENERATED from the pack by `node scripts/stickers.js` and holds each one's SVG verbatim plus the font block once, and `main.js` puts them in. Regenerate, do not edit; `npm run lint:page` fails if the module is stale. Where a sticker goes, when it is there and how long the one that opens waits after the boot is written on its anchor in `index.html`, and the stylesheet's block on `#slaps` says which corner of the glass is free of which instrument when. A sticker never sits where an instrument is at the same moment, never near the bottom at the close, and never carries a number the copy beside it could disagree with.

**The slap is an animation and it is the point of them.** `@keyframes slap` in `index.html`: in from nearly twice the size and eight degrees off, easing IN because that is what a hand does, short onto the glass at 93 per cent with the shadow tightening from 24 px of blur to 4, one ring back, settled. A transition cannot describe an impact, which is what it was before. It must never carry a fill mode, or it outranks the hover rule for the life of the page. Eight is the count because twelve read as a layer of the page rather than as something somebody stuck on it. Two stickers are never in one spot at the same time, and the three spots a phone folds into its one free band are disjoint in T as a set; `npm run lint:page` checks both, and the reduced motion contract has the usual two halves, the stylesheet stilling the animation and `REDUCED` skipping the wait.

**The partners are on the front door the way F1 shows its partners, and nowhere else.** Global Drone Solutions, Mantis FPV and the West Coast Multirotor Club are the simulator's `src/partners/roster.js`, copied into `src/sim/partners/`, and their marks are the roster's one colour cream files under `assets/partners/`, never the colour ones: in the chrome every partner mark is one colour and one height over a small label saying what they are, and the livery is for the simulator's walls. On the paper of the first screen the same files are inked by a filter, which keeps them one colour. They appear in five places. A strip over act one's wordmark, the first thing on the first screen, under "Official partners", which the owner asked for to make them more prominent, beside the same strip by the board's wordmark. A row in the footer above "Also by", in its furniture, ending in "Partner with WebFPV" and the owner's own address, which they asked for. And one moment each in the film, tied to what they do and never two at once: the shop at the foot of the Build order panel (it leaves with it, and drops out with it under 1100 px), the club under the lap clock inked as the OSD is, on for the race only (`osdPartner` in `main.js`, never on a phone), and the school under "You must practise.", because that is its line. None is in a beat. Every mark links to the partner's card on the board's partners page through `data-dest`, and a "Partners" link sits in the footer links; the only links out to a partner are on that page, where they are counted. `src/support-boot.js` counts each mark seen (half of it in the window, drawn, for a second) and clicked, as `landing_hero`, `landing_row` or `landing_moment`, through `src/stats.js`, which sends nothing for Global Privacy Control. A new partner is a roster entry recopied, a mark in the row, and a moment argued for, not a mark wherever there is room. Betaflight's logo is not on this page. It stood beside the partners in the strip and the row under "Powered by" for a day, and the owner took it off on 2026-09-27: Betaflight gave permission to use its name and logo in the game because the project is free, said it has nothing to do with WebFPV and will not publicise it, and holds the trade marks (the simulator's `NOTICE`), and this page is the one that publicises the project. The name stays in the page's words wherever the page says what the control loop is, because that is a fact about the code and the licence's attribution; it is never called a partner, sponsor or supporter, and nothing on the page may suggest Betaflight endorses WebFPV.

**The marks under "Also by Mat Harvey" belong to other sites.** They are other people's artwork in a block that says so, and Winmarchy's green is the one colour on the page that is not in the palette above. That is deliberate. They carry no `data-dest`, so the local origin retargeting in `main.js` never touches them.

**A deploy that changes a script changes its address.** Pages reach a browser with a ten minute cache, but every script and picture comes through the domain's edge with four hours (the simulator's `DEPLOY.md` measured it), so a returning visitor gets the new stylesheet over the old script, and on 2026-09-27 that put the manga page's inked text over the old dark studio with no panels. Until the edge's Browser Cache TTL is fixed, a module that changes in a deploy gets a new `?v=` on every address it is loaded by: the entry in `index.html`, its `modulepreload`, and the import that names it (`src/main.js`, `src/titles.js`, `./stage.js`, `./stickers-data.js`, the wiki's `src/wiki/boot.js`, and the wiki's `wiki.js`, `articles.js`, `cli.js`, `figures.js` and `src/fc/catalog.js` carry one now, and a stamp already shipped is bumped, never reused). A module new in a deploy needs none, and one imported under two addresses runs twice, so every importer of a stamped module is stamped together. `src/titles.js` has two, the film's `index.html` and the wiki's `src/wiki/boot.js`, so its stamp changes in both, and `src/fc/catalog.js` has three, `wiki.js`, `cli.js` and `figures.js`.

**The patch notes end in a velocity graph, and the graph is measured, not typed.** The owner asked on 2026-09-29 for a graph at the foot of the patch notes that shows how the code has grown. It is the last section of `notes/index.html`, between two marker comments, and it is GENERATED: `node scripts/velocity.js ../WebFPVSimulator ../WebFPVSimulator-LeaderBoard`, after a `git fetch origin main` in each of the two checkouts, neither shallow, writes `notes/velocity.json` and the block. Regenerate, do not edit either. It draws two things from the git history of the simulator, the board and this site: lines of source at the end of each day, stacked, and commits a day. "Source" is hand written `.js`, `.mjs`, `.c`, `.h`, `.html` and `.css`, and the JSON lists what is not counted and why: the vendored town, files a script writes, copies of the simulator's files, records, pictures, the wiki's generated pages, and the notes page itself, which would count its own graph. The measure is exact. Each repository's first parent chain, summed by day, equals a direct count of its files at the head, and the script stops if it does not; a shallow clone is refused, because a truncated history is a wrong graph that looks right. The stack order and the colours are fixed: the simulator is mint, the board amber and this site sakura, the dataviz palette's dark steps for aqua, yellow and magenta, validated against the page. Mint and sakura must never touch, because to a viewer with deuteranopia they are one colour, so the board sits between them. Every word on the graph is HTML at a fixed size and only its marks scale; do not put text back inside the SVG, where at 430 px it shrinks to 7 px. The graph reads without `notes/velocity.js`, which adds hover, touch and the arrow keys, and the table view holds every number. That script is loaded by the notes page alone and shipped without a stamp, being new; its first change gives its script tag a `?v=`, as the rule above says, because the page reaches a browser in ten minutes and the script in four hours. It does nothing if the table and the graph disagree, so a stale copy is a graph without hover, not a wrong one. The request arrived cut off after "growth of the code based", so how the code is counted is a reading, stated in the graph's own caption. If the owner meant something else, the caption and `scripts/velocity.js` are where it changes.

## Style

- Plain JavaScript. No TypeScript, no framework, no state library.
- Prefer one file doing an obvious thing over three files doing a clever thing.
- No em dashes or en dashes in prose, comments, commit messages or documentation. Use a comma, colon or full stop. The page's inline separator is `&#183;`.
- Long explanatory comments that say why, not what. Match the voice already in `index.html` and `src/main.js`.

## Working rules

- There is no test suite here. `npm run serve` and look at the page, at a wide width and at 430 px, and at the 900 px breakpoint where the ledger and the instruments drop out. The wiki has the same breakpoint, where its rail becomes a drawer.
- `npm run lint:wiki` before handing the wiki over. It is cheap, and it catches the things that stay invisible until somebody scrolls to the one page that has them.
- `npm run lint:page` before handing the film or the sticker pack over. Also cheap: the share cards, the sitemap, the dashes, and whether `src/stickers-data.js` is what the pack would generate now.
- A patch notes entry regenerates the velocity graph in the same commit: `git fetch origin main` in the simulator's and the board's checkouts, then `node scripts/velocity.js ../WebFPVSimulator ../WebFPVSimulator-LeaderBoard`. `npm run lint:page` fails the day it is forgotten, and `npm test` runs the generator on a year of days and the hover layer on the page. Look at the graph at a wide width and at 430 px after a change to it, because the lint cannot see a label on top of another.
- Never report a check as passing without having run it in the same turn. If a check was not run, say so, say why, and say what was done instead.
- The simulator's `npm run verify` is expensive and does not cover this repository. Do not reach for it here.

## Review

- **Do not run adversarial review, multi agent review or a review workflow unless directed.** Read your own diff, look at the page, and hand the work over. Fan out only when the request asks for it.
- When a review does run, its findings are written down whether or not they were acted on, and a finding that was declined is recorded with the reason.
